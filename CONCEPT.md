## A consciously bilingual human–machine book

## In short

The text of our time is already bilingual. Living human words and computed machine words are woven into it together. Two write it — a person and a machine — and two read it. The Bush Book is a book built on this consciously: in each of its chapters the seam between the two languages is visible, there is a passport for the machine reader, and its sources and outflows are shown. And it grows not like a train, but like a bush.

---

## 1. The phenomenon

### The internet is one book

The internet is one enormous book that human–machine understanding has not yet recognised as a book. It has explicit links and implicit ones. Explicit links are placed by a person: "look there". Implicit links — connections by meaning that nobody placed — are found by a machine. This book, too, has a forked tongue.

### Every book is a root system

The reference list of an article is a set of thought tunnels. They dissolve the borders of a published article or a digitised book. The formal border of a book is a dotted line drawn by its author. In fact every book is a vast root system of connections that have grown through it. It has sources, where its thoughts flowed in from, and outflows, where they flow on. The author's dotted line is a border of responsibility, not a border of meaning.

### The text of our time is bilingual

The single language of a text — English, Russian, Chinese — is an illusion. A modern text is stitched and darned together from two languages: human and machine.

For a person, a word carries the price of an error: what happens next to them and to those close to them depends on it. For a machine, a word is a label attached to the result of a computation. From the outside these are the same words.

Every day billions of words are written together with machines. Each such text is a patchwork quilt sewn from living and computed pieces. The finished text hides the seam.

### Two authors, one who answers

In fact such a text has two authors: a person and a machine. In law there is one author — the person. Only a person can answer for what is written, whether the text was carved in stone, typed on a typewriter or written together with AI.

We hide the machine ourselves: in the shadow of "the editor", in the line "prepared with the help of AI", in the Methods section. These are temporary props. Norms for a text with two authors of different natures do not exist yet.

### The phenomenon will not go away

Whatever we call it — assistance, editing, a tool — it does not change the anatomy and physiology of the text. Future models will learn from this human–machine layer. One can look away; the phenomenon will not disappear. It has to be recognised and used purposefully and consciously.

## 2. What the Bush Book is

The Bush Book is a book deliberately built the way every book of our time is already built, unnoticed.

- **A centre and three borders.** At the centre is the root idea. Around it are three borders: the author's responsibility; the formal border of the published text; the invisible border of links — its sources and outflows.
- **A bush, not a train.** One root, branches growing in different directions. Each chapter can be read on its own. The contours are blurred; new branches may appear. Chapter numbers are a guide, not a reading order.
- **Latent chapters.** Chapters come out now, one at a time, as articles. Each has its own frozen DOI. In fact they are chapters of a book that is being woven as it goes.
- **Conscious bilingualism.** Each chapter has a seam map: where the person's words are, where the machine's formulations are, and where the person stopped the idea from drifting toward the familiar.
- **Two poles.** Each chapter has a passport for the machine reader — a kind of textual barcode. The passport adds nothing to the text. It only shows what is already woven in.
- **Three publication formats.** For the person — the living text. Mixed — the text with its seam map. For the machine — the passport and the links.
- **Visible growth.** The map of the bush is released in new versions. Its permanent DOI is the DOI of the book.

## 3. Why it matters

- **For people.** To see which words in a text are yours and which are computed. To hold on to your own idea instead of handing it over to a smooth answer. This is a skill — seam literacy. It can be taught at school and at university. A textbook in which you can see where the person speaks and where the machine does teaches not to hide the seam, but to read it.
- **For future models.** To learn from text in which the difference between the living and the computed has not been erased. The most valuable places in such text are those where a person corrected the machine: there, a new idea did not become a familiar one.
- **For authors and readers.** Everyone's contribution is shown honestly. Nobody has to be added "just in case", and nobody has to be hidden.

## 4. Map of the bush, version 0.1

**Root.** Under the same words — two languages. The text of our time is already bilingual and two-poled.

**Branches — latent chapters:**

1. **Word.** Two languages under the same words. Chapter "The Snake's Tongue" — in progress.
2. **Conversation.** Why a new idea drifts toward a familiar one. A machine can bring an idea to light — or overexpose it. Also "The Snake's Tongue".
3. **Text.** A quilt of two languages. Two authors in fact, one in responsibility.
4. **Reader.** The machine is now the main reader, but it is the postman, not the addressee. Three publication formats.
5. **Layer.** Future models learn from hybrid texts. The essay "The Blind Kittens and the Trainers of Their New AI Models" (published) belongs here.
6. **School.** Seam literacy.

**Frame.** A history of transitions: speech, writing, print, the typewriter, the hybrid text. Each transition gave the text a new "passport": the manuscript colophon, the title page, copyright, the DOI.

**Practical line.** The specifications "Conditions of Admission and Participation for People Testing and Raising AI Models" and "A Registry of Proto-Concepts with Referent Quarantine" (published). A future format for the passport and the seam map.

## 5. Neighbours and the boundary of novelty

| Neighbour | What it already does | What it lacks |
|---|---|---|
| Intertextuality — Kristeva, Barthes (1960s) | Every text is a mosaic, a tissue of quotations | The machine as co-author and reader |
| Borges, "The Library of Babel" (1941); Bush, the Memex (1945); Nelson, the docuverse (1960s) | The world of texts as one linked book | A machine language inside the text |
| The networked book — Institute for the Future of the Book, Bob Stein | A book that is never finished and lives in an ecology of readers and authors | The machine is only a carrier |
| "Pharmako-AI", K Allado-McDowell (2020) | A book written with GPT-3; human and machine text set in different typefaces | The seam is visible because it is a dialogue; not inside continuous prose; no passport, no links |
| Nanopublications — Groth, Gibson, Velterop (2010) | Scientific assertions in machine-readable form, with provenance | A living book for which this is the second half |
| Colophon (GitHub), HaLLMark (CHI 2024), Grammarly Authorship, IBM AI Attribution Toolkit (2025) | Recording and disclosing human and AI contribution | They answer "who wrote it", not "which language is this word from"; the record stays inside the tool |
| SynthID-Text — Google DeepMind (Nature, 2024) | An invisible watermark in machine text | Only the machine tip of one vendor is marked |
| 1EdTech (2025) | Labels for AI content in learning materials, five levels | A label on the whole item, not a map inside the text |
| LessWrong | Special blocks for model-written text | A site rule, not the structure of a book |
| Mixed-text detectors — MixSet (2024), Zeng et al. (2024) | Finding machine parts after the fact | Unreliable: the seam is better marked while writing |

**What is new in the Bush Book.** All of this is joined deliberately in one book. The seam is shown inside continuous prose. Each chapter has a machine passport. Sources and outflows are recorded as machine-readable links. The form is a bush of latent chapters. And underneath is the idea that the text of our time is already bilingual, and the Bush Book is a working model of that phenomenon. This holds as far as the author's search went; no search is complete.

---

## 6. What this note is not

- It is not an AI detector and not a way to catch cheating.
- It is not a call to ban or restrict AI. The author uses it every day and is grateful to the people who build these systems.
- It is not a claim that a machine has thoughts, feelings or consciousness. Human words about the machine appear here only in quotation marks.
- It is not a demand to recognise the machine as a legal author. The person answers.
- It is not a finished book. It is the map of a growing bush.

## Appendix A. Structure

*For machines, and for anyone who wants to build their own Bush Book.*
"MUST", "SHOULD" and "MAY" mean: required, recommended, optional.

### A.1. Chapter

Each chapter is a package of three parts:

1. **Text** — for the person.
2. **Seam map** — mixed format: the same text with markup, plus a glossary of inlays.
3. **Passport** — for the machine.

All three parts MUST say the same thing. The passport describes the text; it does not replace it.

### A.2. Seam map

- Plain type — connecting text; state whose it is.
- **Bold** — the author's living words.
- *Italic* — a machine's formulations; state which machine.
- `Monospace` or colour — a second machine's formulations, if there was one.
- **Corrections** SHOULD be marked separately: places where the person stopped the idea from drifting toward the familiar.
- Word-level precision is not the point. The map shows the weave; it is not an investigation. Differences between the author's markup and the machine's markup are data too.

### A.3. Chapter passport

Fields:

- the thesis in one sentence;
- what the text is not;
- terms with definitions, each marked with whose vocabulary it belongs to: human, machine or observer;
- claims with a status: observation, hypothesis, confirmed by a source;
- the seam: whose key formulations are whose;
- sources;
- outflows (added in new versions).

Example:

```yaml
chapter: "The Snake's Tongue"
part_of: "The Bush Book"          # DOI of the map of the bush
thesis: "Under the same words, humans and machines speak two languages."
is_not:
  - "not about AI making mistakes that need checking"
  - "not about machine consciousness"
terms:
  - term: "drift toward the familiar"
    vocabulary: observer          # human | machine | observer
    definition: "a new idea is replaced by a similar, more frequent one"
claims:
  - text: "in long conversations models settle early on what the person wants"
    status: confirmed            # observation | hypothesis | confirmed
    source: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120"
seam:
  human: ["a thought without weight", "Clamp!"]
  machine: {claude: ["the fork"], chatgpt: ["Cold does not accept explanations"]}
sources: ["10.5281/zenodo.22924768"]
outflows: []
```

### A.4. Links

Links are recorded in the Zenodo record's metadata with standard DataCite relation types, which machines read.

| What is linked | Relation |
|---|---|
| chapter → map of the bush | IsPartOf |
| map of the bush → chapter | HasPart |
| chapter → a source it draws on | References |
| chapter → the work it grew out of | IsDerivedFrom |
| chapter → the next chapter of the same branch | IsContinuedBy (inverse: Continues) |
| chapter → a work that cites it (outflow) | IsCitedBy |
| passport or seam map → chapter text | IsSupplementTo |

### A.5. Map of the bush

- A separate record on Zenodo.
- Each new chapter is a new version of the map.
- The permanent DOI of all versions (the concept DOI) is the DOI of the book.
- Links to already published works MAY be added by editing their record metadata, without changing their files.

### A.6. Website

- A chapter page: the text and a visible "What this is about / What it is not about" block.
- Hidden schema.org description: type ScholarlyArticle; isPartOf — the book; author — a Person with an ORCID. citation_* meta tags for Google Scholar.
- Bots MUST see what people see. Showing them something else is cloaking.
- Each page links to the chapter's DOI and to the map of the bush.

### A.7. Three vocabularies

Human, machine, observer. Human words about the machine appear only in quotation marks: it "thinks", "wants", "fears". Machine terms — "weight", "neighbouring idea" — are not carried over to the person.
